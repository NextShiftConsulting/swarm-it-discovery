# Causal Interpretation of Neural Network Computations with Contribution Decomposition

Title: Causal Interpretation of Neural Network Computations through Contribution Decomposition

1. **Core Contribution**:
This paper tackles the crucial challenge of understanding how neural networks transform inputs into outputs, which is essential for interpreting and manipulating their behavior. The key innovation is the introduction of CODEC (Contribution Decomposition), a method that uses sparse autoencoders to decompose a network's behavior into sparse motifs of hidden-neuron contributions. This approach allows the authors to reveal causal processes that cannot be determined by analyzing activations alone, providing a richer and more interpretable framework for understanding how nonlinear computations evolve across hierarchical layers in neural networks.

The significance of this work lies in its ability to go beyond merely identifying hidden-layer activation patterns correlated with human-interpretable concepts. By directly examining how hidden neurons contribute to driving network outputs, CODEC offers a more direct and insightful path to uncovering the causal mechanisms underlying neural network computations. This is a critical step towards building more transparent and interpretable AI systems.

2. **Technical Approach**:
The authors introduce CODEC, a novel method that leverages sparse autoencoders to decompose a neural network's behavior into sparse motifs of hidden-neuron contributions. The key idea is to represent the network's input-output mapping as a sparse set of "contribution modes" that capture the causal relationships between hidden neurons and network outputs.

Specifically, CODEC works as follows:
- It trains a sparse autoencoder to learn a compact representation of the network's input-output mapping, where the autoencoder's hidden layer captures the contribution modes.
- The contribution modes represent sparse patterns of hidden-neuron activations that are predictive of the network's outputs.
- By analyzing the contribution modes, the authors can uncover the causal processes driving the network's computations, revealing insights that are not accessible through activation-based analysis alone.

The authors apply CODEC to both benchmark image-classification networks and state-of-the-art models of neural activity in the vertebrate retina, demonstrating the versatility and insights offered by their approach.

3. **Key Results**:
Applying CODEC to benchmark image-classification networks, the authors make several key discoveries:
- Contributions grow in sparsity and dimensionality across layers, indicating an increase in the complexity of causal processes.
- Unexpectedly, the positive and negative effects of hidden neurons on network outputs progressively decorrelate, suggesting a sophisticated computational strategy.
- Decomposing contributions into sparse modes enables greater control and interpretation of intermediate layers, supporting both causal manipulations of network output and human-interpretable visualizations of distinct image components that drive the network's decisions.

When analyzing state-of-the-art models of neural activity in the vertebrate retina, CODEC uncovers combinatorial actions of model interneurons and identifies the sources of dynamic receptive fields, providing mechanistic insights that were previously inaccessible.

4. **Significance & Limitations**:
The significance of this work lies in its ability to provide a more direct and interpretable understanding of how neural networks compute. By decomposing network behavior into sparse contribution modes, CODEC offers a window into the causal processes that drive network outputs, going beyond the limitations of activation-based analysis. This is a crucial step towards building more transparent and trustworthy AI systems, as it allows for greater control and manipulation of a network's internal computations.

A potential limitation of this work is that the CODEC approach is still based on a specific architectural choice (sparse autoencoders) and may not be generalizable to all types of neural network architectures. Additionally, the interpretability of the contribution modes may be dependent on the quality and granularity of the extracted features. Future research could explore alternative decomposition techniques or investigate the integration of CODEC with other interpretability methods to further enhance the understanding of neural network computations.
