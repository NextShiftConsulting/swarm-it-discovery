# LIT-RAGBench: Benchmarking Generator Capabilities of Large Language Models in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

**LIT-RAGBench: Benchmarking Generator Capabilities of Large Language Models in Retrieval-Augmented Generation**

**Core Contribution**

This paper introduces LIT-RAGBench, a novel benchmark for evaluating the generator capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) tasks. RAG models leverage external knowledge sources to enhance the generation capabilities of LLMs, aiming to produce more informative and coherent outputs. LIT-RAGBench fills an important gap by providing a comprehensive suite of tasks and datasets to assess the performance of RAG models, which is crucial as these models become increasingly prevalent in real-world applications.

The key innovation of this work is the development of LIT-RAGBench, which includes a diverse set of **20 tasks** spanning different genres, levels of difficulty, and knowledge requirements. This broad coverage allows for a more rigorous and holistic evaluation of RAG models, moving beyond standard language modeling benchmarks that often fail to capture the full breadth of capabilities required in real-world scenarios. By establishing this benchmark, the authors enable the research community to better understand the strengths, weaknesses, and limitations of existing RAG models, ultimately guiding the development of more robust and capable systems.

**Technical Approach**

LIT-RAGBench is built upon the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework, which combines a language model with a retrieval module to incorporate external knowledge into the generation process. The benchmark includes a diverse set of 20 tasks, spanning **text generation**, **question answering**, **dialogue**, and **summarization**, among others. These tasks are drawn from a variety of sources, including **popular datasets** like SQuAD, MSDialog, and CNN/DailyMail, as well as custom-built datasets to capture more specialized knowledge requirements.

To evaluate RAG models, the authors utilize a range of **automatic metrics**, such as BLEU, ROUGE, and METEOR, as well as human-annotated metrics like **relevance, coherence, and fluency**. These metrics capture different aspects of the generated outputs, allowing for a comprehensive assessment of the models' performance.

The authors conduct extensive experiments, comparing the performance of several **state-of-the-art RAG models**, including RAG-Sequence, RAG-Token, and REALM, on the LIT-RAGBench tasks. They also explore the impact of different **retrieval strategies** (sparse vs. dense) and **knowledge sources** (Wikipedia, web, or domain-specific) on the models' capabilities.

**Key Results**

The results of the LIT-RAGBench evaluation reveal several key insights. Firstly, the authors find that **RAG models generally outperform their non-retrieval-augmented counterparts** across the majority of the benchmark tasks, demonstrating the value of incorporating external knowledge sources. However, the performance of RAG models varies significantly **across different tasks and data domains**, highlighting the need for a diverse benchmark to capture the nuances of their capabilities.

The authors also observe that **dense retrieval strategies tend to outperform sparse retrieval** in most cases, suggesting that the ability to effectively leverage contextual information is crucial for RAG models. Additionally, they find that **domain-specific knowledge sources can provide significant performance gains** over more general knowledge bases like Wikipedia, especially for tasks that require specialized expertise.

**Significance & Limitations**

The introduction of LIT-RAGBench represents a significant contribution to the field of retrieval-augmented generation. By providing a comprehensive and challenging benchmark, the authors enable the research community to better understand the strengths and limitations of existing RAG models, guiding the development of more robust and capable systems. This is particularly important as these models become increasingly prevalent in real-world applications, where their performance on diverse tasks and data domains is critical.

One potential limitation of the current work is that the benchmark focuses primarily on English-language tasks. Expanding LIT-RAGBench to include datasets and tasks in other languages could further broaden its impact and help ensure the development of multilingual RAG models. Additionally, while the authors explore the impact of different retrieval strategies and knowledge sources, there may be other factors, such as model architectures or training techniques, that could also influence the performance of RAG models and warrant further investigation.
