# Leveraging LLM Parametric Knowledge for Fact Checking without Retrieval

## Core Contribution

This paper tackles the important challenge of enhancing the **trustworthiness** of large language models (LLMs) by developing a novel approach for **fact-checking** that does not rely on external knowledge retrieval. Existing fact-checking methods are constrained by the availability and quality of retrieved evidence, while failing to fully leverage the intrinsic fact-verification capabilities of LLMs. 

The key innovation of this work is the introduction of the **fact-checking without retrieval** task, which focuses on verifying the factuality of arbitrary natural language claims, regardless of their source. The authors propose a comprehensive evaluation framework to assess the generalization of such systems across various dimensions, including long-tail knowledge, claim source variation, multilinguality, and long-form generation. This represents a significant advancement over traditional fact-checking approaches and lays the groundwork for developing more scalable and versatile LLM-based fact-verification systems.

## Technical Approach

To study the fact-checking without retrieval task, the authors introduce a diverse set of 9 datasets spanning multiple languages and domains. These datasets cover a wide range of claim types, from web content and human-written text to model-generated outputs. The authors then evaluate 18 different methods across 3 LLM architectures (BERT, RoBERTa, and T5) to assess their performance on this task.

The key innovation in the authors' technical approach is the **INTRA** method, which stands for "Interactions-based Fact Checking". INTRA leverages the **internal representations** of the LLM, rather than solely relying on the output logits, to capture the complex interactions between different components of the model's knowledge. This allows INTRA to better exploit the intrinsic fact-verification capabilities of the LLM, going beyond simple output-based approaches.

The authors conduct a thorough experimental evaluation, comparing INTRA to various baselines, including **logit-based** methods that directly use the model's output probabilities for fact-checking. Additionally, they assess the models' robustness to challenging scenarios, such as claims with long-tail knowledge, cross-domain generalization, and multilingual settings.

## Key Results

The authors' experiments reveal that **logit-based approaches often underperform compared to methods that leverage the LLM's internal representations**, such as INTRA. This finding suggests that the intrinsic knowledge encoded in the model's internal structure can be more effectively exploited for fact-checking than relying solely on the output probabilities.

INTRA, the authors' proposed method, achieves **state-of-the-art performance** on the fact-checking without retrieval task across the 9 evaluated datasets. Notably, INTRA demonstrates strong **generalization capabilities**, maintaining high performance in challenging settings like long-tail knowledge, cross-domain claims, and multilingual scenarios.

## Significance & Limitations

This work establishes fact-checking without retrieval as a **promising research direction** that can complement traditional retrieval-based approaches. By leveraging the intrinsic knowledge of LLMs, this method can improve the **scalability** and **versatility** of fact-verification systems, enabling their use as reward signals during training or as components integrated into generation processes.

While the authors present compelling results, some potential limitations and future research directions are worth noting. The evaluation could be further expanded to include even more diverse claim sources, such as social media posts or user-generated content, which may pose additional challenges. Additionally, investigating the **interpretability** of the INTRA method and understanding the specific knowledge representations it exploits could lead to valuable insights for enhancing LLM trustworthiness.
