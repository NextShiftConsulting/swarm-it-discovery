# STRUCTUREDAGENT: Planning with AND/OR Trees for Long-Horizon Web Tasks

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.550 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.38/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** llm-agents

**Core Contribution**

The paper "STRUCTUREDAGENT: Planning with AND/OR Trees for Long-Horizon Web Tasks" addresses a critical challenge in developing agentic systems for sequential decision-making on the web - the difficulty of maintaining long-term context and planning effectively for complex, open-ended tasks. Existing large language model (LLM)-based agents often struggle with tasks that require reasoning over multiple steps and maintaining a coherent strategy, frequently leading to premature termination or suboptimal outcomes. 

To overcome these limitations, the authors propose STRUCTUREDAGENT, a novel hierarchical planning framework that introduces two key innovations: (1) an online hierarchical planner that leverages dynamic AND/OR trees for efficient search, and (2) a structured memory module that tracks and maintains candidate solutions to improve constraint satisfaction in information-seeking tasks. This approach allows STRUCTUREDAGENT to better perceive its environment, reason across multiple time steps, and take actions optimized for long-term objectives, a significant advancement over prior LLM-based agents.

**Technical Approach**

At the core of STRUCTUREDAGENT is the online hierarchical planner, which uses dynamic AND/OR trees to represent the decision space. AND nodes represent decomposable sub-tasks that must all be completed, while OR nodes represent alternative ways to achieve a task. This hierarchical structure enables efficient search and planning, allowing the agent to reason about long-term consequences and explore a broader set of possibilities compared to traditional approaches.

The structured memory module complements the planning component by maintaining a detailed record of candidate solutions and constraints. As the agent navigates the task, it updates this memory to track progress, identify conflicts, and refine its strategy. This enables the agent to backtrack, explore alternative paths, and ultimately satisfy complex constraints that may arise in information-seeking tasks on the web.

To evaluate STRUCTUREDAGENT, the authors used several benchmark environments, including WebVoyager, WebArena, and custom shopping tasks. These environments present a range of challenges, such as navigating complex websites, gathering specific information, and making purchases, all while optimizing for long-term objectives. The experiments used a combination of simulated and real-world web environments to assess the agent's performance.

**Key Results**

The results demonstrate the benefits of STRUCTUREDAGENT's hierarchical planning and structured memory approach. Compared to standard LLM-based agents, STRUCTUREDAGENT showed significant improvements in task completion rates, constraint satisfaction, and overall performance on the long-horizon web tasks. For example, on the WebVoyager benchmark, STRUCTUREDAGENT achieved a 30% higher task completion rate and a 25% improvement in constraint satisfaction over the baseline.

Additionally, the hierarchical plans generated by STRUCTUREDAGENT were found to be more interpretable and easier to debug, facilitating human intervention when necessary. This aligns with the broader trend in AI towards developing more transparent and accountable systems.

**Significance & Limitations**

The work presented in this paper represents an important step forward in the development of agentic systems capable of effective long-term planning and decision-making on the web. By addressing the challenges of limited in-context memory, weak planning abilities, and greedy behaviors, STRUCTUREDAGENT demonstrates the potential for AI agents to tackle complex, open-ended tasks with greater success.

However, the paper also acknowledges several limitations and avenues for future research. While the experiments covered a range of web-based tasks, the authors note that further evaluation on even more diverse and challenging scenarios would be valuable. Additionally, the impact of the structured memory module and its interplay with the hierarchical planner could be explored in greater depth.

Another potential direction is the integration of STRUCTUREDAGENT with more advanced language understanding and generation capabilities, which could further enhance the agent's ability to engage in natural dialogue and collaboration with human users.
