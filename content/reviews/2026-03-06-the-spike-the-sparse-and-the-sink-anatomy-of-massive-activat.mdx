# The Spike, the Sparse and the Sink: Anatomy of Massive Activations and Attention Sinks

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.550 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.37/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** representation-learning

Title: Unveiling the Anatomy of Massive Activations and Attention Sinks in Transformer Language Models

Core Contribution:
This paper delves into two intriguing phenomena observed in Transformer language models: **massive activations** and **attention sinks**. Massive activations refer to a small number of tokens exhibiting extreme outliers in a few channels, while attention sinks describe certain tokens attracting disproportionate attention mass, regardless of semantic relevance. The authors systematically investigate the relationship between these two phenomena, which have been previously observed to frequently co-occur, but whose functional roles and causal relationship remained unclear. Through a series of insightful experiments, the researchers uncover the underlying architectural factors that enable the co-occurrence of these two phenomena and elucidate their distinct yet related functions within the Transformer models.

The key finding of this paper is that the co-occurrence of massive activations and attention sinks is largely an artifact of the modern Transformer design, particularly the **pre-norm configuration**. The authors show that by ablating this architectural choice, the two phenomena can be decoupled, shedding light on their individual roles and mechanisms. This work is important as it provides a deeper understanding of the inner workings of Transformer models, which have become ubiquitous in natural language processing and beyond.

Technical Approach:
The researchers conducted a systematic set of experiments to investigate the relationship between massive activations and attention sinks. They utilized a variety of Transformer-based language models, including GPT-2, BERT, and T5, across different datasets and tasks.

To study massive activations, the authors analyzed the distribution of activations in the hidden representations of the models, identifying the tokens that exhibited extreme outliers in a small number of channels. For attention sinks, they measured the attention mass attracted by each token, regardless of its semantic relevance.

Through their experiments, the researchers carefully examined the architectural choices that enable the co-occurrence of these two phenomena. They focused on the pre-norm configuration, which is a common design choice in modern Transformer models that involves applying layer normalization before the main transformation layers. By ablating this pre-norm configuration, the authors were able to decouple the massive activations and attention sinks, shedding light on their distinct functional roles.

Key Results:
The key findings of this study are:
- The co-occurrence of massive activations and attention sinks is largely an artifact of the pre-norm configuration in Transformer models.
- Massive activations operate globally, inducing near-constant hidden representations that persist across layers, effectively functioning as implicit parameters of the model.
- Attention sinks operate locally, modulating attention outputs across heads and biasing individual heads toward short-range dependencies.
- Ablating the pre-norm configuration causes the two phenomena to decouple, revealing their distinct roles and mechanisms.

Significance and Limitations:
This work is significant as it provides a deeper understanding of the internal mechanisms of Transformer models, which have become ubiquitous in natural language processing and beyond. By elucidating the relationship between massive activations and attention sinks, the authors shed light on the architectural choices that shape the behavior of these models.

While the paper offers valuable insights, it also raises new questions and potential limitations. For instance, the authors do not explore the implications of these phenomena for the overall performance and capabilities of Transformer models. Additionally, the study is primarily focused on language models, and it would be interesting to investigate whether similar patterns emerge in Transformers applied to other domains, such as vision or multimodal tasks.
