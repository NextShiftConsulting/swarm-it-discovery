# SUREON: A Benchmark and Vision-Language-Model for Surgical Reasoning

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.495 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.37/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** ai-safety

**Core Contribution**

This paper introduces SUREON, a large-scale video QA dataset that captures the expert surgical reasoning and decision-making skills that are currently lacking in surgical AI systems. While existing computer vision models can detect surgical instruments and procedures, they lack the higher-level understanding of intent, risk, and anticipation that experienced surgeons possess. SUREON addresses this gap by systematically harvesting and structuring the explanatory narrations found in surgical video lectures, which encode the rich reasoning that experts use to guide their actions.

The key innovation of SUREON is its multi-agent pipeline for extracting 206.8k surgical QA pairs across 134.7k video clips and 170 procedure types. This dataset provides a benchmark for evaluating the surgical reasoning capabilities of AI models, going beyond simple perception tasks to assess an AI's understanding of surgical rationale, safety assessments, and forecasting. The authors also introduce two new models - SureonVLM and SureonVLM-R1 - which leverage this dataset to substantially outperform larger general-domain models on both the SUREON benchmark and standard surgical perception tasks. This is a significant step towards developing AI systems that can truly understand and assist with complex surgical decision-making.

**Technical Approach**

The SUREON dataset is constructed by harvesting explanatory narrations from surgical video lectures, which are then processed through a multi-agent pipeline to extract structured question-answer pairs. The first agent uses speech recognition to transcribe the narrations, while a second agent uses natural language processing to identify relevant QA pairs based on 12 predefined categories, such as "safety assessment", "decision rationale", and "forecasting". A third agent then validates the QA pairs, resulting in a high-quality benchmark of 354 examples.

To evaluate the surgical reasoning capabilities enabled by SUREON, the authors introduce two new models. SureonVLM is a vision-language model that is fine-tuned on the SUREON dataset, allowing it to understand and reason about the visual and textual information in surgical videos. SureonVLM-R1 is a reasoning-focused model that is trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization, a technique that encourages the model to learn explicit reasoning behaviors.

Both models are evaluated on the SUREON benchmark, as well as standard surgical perception tasks like instrument detection and phase recognition. The authors compare the performance of these specialized models to larger general-domain models, such as CLIP and GPT-3, to demonstrate the value of the SUREON dataset and the models' surgical reasoning capabilities.

**Key Results**

The authors report that both SureonVLM and SureonVLM-R1 substantially outperform the larger general-domain models on the SUREON benchmark, achieving over 84% accuracy. This suggests that the SUREON dataset and the specialized models' training process successfully capture the expert surgical reasoning that is lacking in more general AI systems.

Qualitative analysis of SureonVLM-R1 further reveals that the model exhibits explicit reasoning behavior, such as inferring operative intent from the visual context. This suggests that the Group Relative Policy Optimization approach used to train the model encourages the development of interpretable reasoning capabilities.

Additionally, the authors show that the SureonVLM and SureonVLM-R1 models also outperform the general-domain models on standard surgical perception tasks, demonstrating the benefits of incorporating surgical reasoning into the models' capabilities.

**Significance & Limitations**

The SUREON dataset and the surgical reasoning models developed in this paper represent a significant advancement in the field of surgical AI. By leveraging the rich explanatory narrations found in surgical video lectures, the authors have created a benchmark that can assess an AI's understanding of the higher-level decision-making and anticipation skills that are critical for successful surgical outcomes.

The success of the SureonVLM and SureonVLM-R1 models in outperforming larger general-domain models highlights the importance of incorporating domain-specific knowledge and reasoning capabilities into AI systems, rather than relying solely on large-scale datasets and general-purpose models.

One potential limitation of this work is the reliance on the quality and completeness of the extracted QA pairs from the surgical video lectures. While the authors have implemented a rigorous validation process, there may still be some noise or gaps in the dataset. Additionally, the benchmark is limited to a specific set of 12 question categories, and it remains to be seen how well the models would generalize to a broader range of surgical reasoning tasks.

Future work could explore ways to further enrich the SUREON dataset, such as incorporating additional sources of surgical knowledge or expanding the question categories. Additionally, the development of models that can seamlessly integrate low-level perception, high-level reasoning, and interactive decision-making would be a valuable next step in advancing surgical AI.
