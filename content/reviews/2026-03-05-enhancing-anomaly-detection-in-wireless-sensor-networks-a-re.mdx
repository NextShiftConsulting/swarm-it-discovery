# Enhancing Anomaly Detection in Wireless Sensor Networks: A Review of Machine Learning and Neural Network-Based Approaches

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.550 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.00/0.00/0.00 | **Topics:** LLM Agents and Reasoning

Enhancing Anomaly Detection in Wireless Sensor Networks: A Review of Machine Learning and Neural Network-Based Approaches

**Core Contribution**

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) play a crucial role in cyber-physical systems, enabling real-time data collection and decision-making for applications like smart cities, environmental monitoring, and automation. However, the unique challenges of WSNs, such as limited computing power, network vulnerabilities, and sensor failures, make traditional centralized anomaly detection approaches unsuitable. This paper presents a comprehensive review of state-of-the-art machine learning and neural network-based techniques for enhancing anomaly detection in WSNs.

The key contribution of this work is the in-depth analysis of neural network and Extreme Learning Machine (ELM)-based anomaly detection models, which are particularly well-suited for the distributed, real-time nature of WSNs. Unlike conventional neural networks, the review highlights that ELM-based methods can perform effectively in time-sensitive environments, making them a promising solution for the dynamic and resource-constrained nature of WSNs.

**Technical Approach**

The authors employed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) framework to conduct a thorough literature review, focusing on publications from 2013 to 2024 in the SCOPUS database. This allowed them to identify and analyze the latest advancements in artificial intelligence for the Internet of Things (AI-IoT), with a particular emphasis on neural networks and ELM-based anomaly detection (ELM-AD) models.

The review covers the unique challenges of anomaly detection in WSNs, such as limited computing power, high vulnerability to network attacks and ecological anomalies, and sensor failures. It then delves into the technical details of neural network and ELM-based approaches, discussing their architectures, training methodologies, and suitability for WSN applications.

Conventional neural networks, while capable of achieving higher detection and classification success rates, often face challenges in terms of the time required for the networks to process the complex data. In contrast, the review highlights that ELM-based methods, with their fast training and execution times, are better suited for the time-sensitive requirements of WSNs.

**Key Results**

The review's findings suggest that ELM-based anomaly detection models outperform traditional neural networks in terms of their effectiveness and efficiency in WSN environments. Specifically, the authors note that ELM-AD models exhibit lower computational complexity, faster training and deployment times, and the ability to adapt to the dynamic and resource-constrained nature of WSNs.

While conventional neural networks may achieve higher detection and classification rates, the review emphasizes that this comes at the cost of increased processing time and energy consumption, which can be critical limitations in WSN applications. In contrast, the ELM-based approaches are able to maintain a high level of accuracy while addressing the unique challenges of the WSN domain.

**Significance & Limitations**

This review is significant because it provides a comprehensive and up-to-date analysis of the state of the art in anomaly detection for WSNs, highlighting the importance of leveraging AI-based solutions to address the inherent challenges of these distributed, real-time systems. By focusing on the comparative strengths of neural networks and ELM-based approaches, the review offers valuable insights for researchers and practitioners working on enhancing the resilience and reliability of WSNs.

One potential limitation of the review is that it does not delve into the specific performance metrics or benchmarks used in the analyzed studies, which could provide a more quantitative assessment of the relative merits of the different techniques. Additionally, the review could have explored the potential trade-offs between detection accuracy and resource-efficiency in more depth, as well as the potential for hybridizing neural network and ELM-based approaches to leverage the strengths of both.
