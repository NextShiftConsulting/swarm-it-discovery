# Judge Reliability Harness: Stress Testing the Reliability of LLM Judges

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.550 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.38/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** ai-safety

Title: Stress Testing the Reliability of Large Language Model (LLM) Judges

Core Contribution:
This paper presents the Judge Reliability Harness, an open-source library for constructing validation suites that rigorously assess the reliability of LLM-based scoring and judgment systems. As LLMs are increasingly used to evaluate performance in AI benchmarks, it is crucial to have robust tooling to evaluate the consistency and robustness of these "judge" models. The key innovation of this work is the development of a comprehensive suite of reliability tests that go beyond simple accuracy metrics to uncover vulnerabilities in how LLM judges respond to perturbations in the input text. This is an important step towards ensuring the reliability and trustworthiness of LLM-powered evaluation systems.

Technical Approach:
The Judge Reliability Harness takes as input a benchmark dataset and an LLM judge configuration, and then generates a battery of reliability tests to evaluate the judge's performance. These tests assess both binary judgment accuracy as well as ordinal grading capabilities across two task formats: free-response and agentic (goal-oriented) tasks. 

The specific reliability tests include:
- Accuracy in judging another LLM's ability to complete a task, with perturbations like text formatting changes, paraphrasing, changes in verbosity, and flipping the ground truth label.
- Stability of ordinal grading under similar perturbations.
- Robustness to adversarial examples designed to confuse the judge.

The authors evaluated four state-of-the-art LLM judges across four benchmark datasets spanning safety, persuasion, misuse, and agentic behavior. These datasets were chosen to cover a diverse range of text-based AI tasks that LLM judges might be applied to in practice.

Key Results:
The authors found meaningful variation in the performance of the LLM judges across the different benchmarks and perturbation types. No single judge was found to be uniformly reliable across all the tests in the harness. For example, the judges showed inconsistencies in accurately judging another LLM's ability to complete a task, with accuracy dropping significantly under simple text formatting changes, paraphrasing, or changes in verbosity.

Additionally, the judges exhibited stability issues in their ordinal grading, with scores fluctuating despite minimal changes to the input text. The authors also found that adversarial examples could substantially confuse the judges, highlighting opportunities to improve the robustness of these systems.

Significance & Limitations:
This work is an important contribution to the growing field of AI safety and robustness. As LLMs become more ubiquitous in AI systems, it is critical to have rigorous tooling to assess their reliability and uncover potential vulnerabilities. The Judge Reliability Harness provides a much-needed framework for stress testing LLM judges, which can help drive the development of more robust and trustworthy evaluation systems.

However, the authors acknowledge that this is an initial exploration, and more work is needed to fully capture the nuances of LLM judge reliability. The current harness focuses on text-based tasks, but future iterations could expand to other modalities, such as vision or multimodal inputs. Additionally, the authors note that the specific perturbations and benchmarks used in this study may not be exhaustive, and further research is needed to identify the most critical failure modes for LLM judges.
