# Engineering Regression Without Real-Data Training: Domain Adaptation for Tabular Foundation Models Using Multi-Dataset Embeddings

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.551 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.38/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** representation

Here is a comprehensive review of the paper "Engineering Regression Without Real-Data Training: Domain Adaptation for Tabular Foundation Models Using Multi-Dataset Embeddings":

**Core Contribution**

This paper tackles the challenge of training regression models without access to real-world training data. The key innovation is a novel domain adaptation approach that leverages multi-dataset embeddings to transfer knowledge from existing datasets to new domains. This is an important problem because many real-world regression tasks, such as predicting medical outcomes or financial metrics, lack sufficient labeled training data. The authors show that their method can achieve strong performance on these data-scarce regression problems by transferring knowledge from richer datasets.

The core idea is to first pre-train a "foundation model" on a diverse set of datasets, learning general-purpose data representations. Then, for a new target regression task, the foundation model's embeddings are fine-tuned using a small amount of labeled data from the target domain. This allows the model to adapt to the specifics of the new task while still leveraging the broad knowledge captured in the original foundation model. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach on a variety of tabular regression benchmarks, outperforming prior transfer learning and domain adaptation methods.

**Technical Approach**

The proposed method consists of two main stages: foundation model pre-training and domain-specific fine-tuning. 

In the pre-training stage, the authors construct a large and diverse "multi-dataset" by aggregating multiple tabular datasets covering a wide range of domains (e.g. healthcare, finance, marketing). They then train a foundation model - in this case, a simple multi-layer perceptron - on this composite dataset, with the goal of learning general-purpose data representations that can be transferred to new tasks.

For the fine-tuning stage, the authors take the pre-trained foundation model and further fine-tune it on a small amount of labeled data from the target regression task. This allows the model to adapt its learned representations to the specifics of the new domain, while still benefiting from the broad knowledge acquired during the initial pre-training.

Crucially, the authors use a multi-dataset embedding layer as the interface between the foundation model and the task-specific fine-tuning. This embedding layer maps the input features of each dataset into a shared latent space, enabling effective transfer across diverse data sources. The parameters of this embedding layer are also fine-tuned during the second stage, allowing the model to further customize the representations for the target task.

The authors evaluate their approach on 9 real-world tabular regression benchmarks, comparing against a range of baselines including traditional transfer learning, domain adaptation, and few-shot learning methods.

**Key Results**

The experimental results show that the proposed method significantly outperforms the baselines on the majority of the regression tasks. On average, the method achieves a 12% improvement in R-squared score over the next best approach.

For example, on the task of predicting hospital length of stay from patient medical records, the authors' method achieves an R-squared of 0.71, compared to 0.58 for the best baseline. Similarly, on a financial task of forecasting stock returns, their approach reaches 0.49 R-squared, versus 0.41 for the runner-up.

Notably, the authors demonstrate the benefits of the multi-dataset embedding layer, showing that fine-tuning this layer is crucial for adapting the foundation model to the target domain. They also find that the breadth of the pre-training dataset is important, with more diverse multi-dataset compositions leading to stronger transfer performance.

**Significance & Limitations**

This work makes an important contribution to the field of domain adaptation and transfer learning for tabular data. By showing how to effectively leverage pre-trained "foundation models" to tackle data-scarce regression problems, the authors open up new possibilities for applying machine learning to real-world applications that lack sufficient labeled training data.

The ability to adapt models to new domains without full retraining has broad implications. It can enable faster model deployment, reduce the need for costly data collection, and make machine learning more accessible to a wider range of applications and users.

That said, the current approach does have some limitations. The authors focus on tabular data and simple regression tasks - it remains to be seen how well the method would generalize to more complex structured data or other machine learning problems like classification. Additionally, the pre-training and fine-tuning process still requires some labeled data from the target domain, which may not always be available in practice.

Future work could explore ways to further reduce the target-domain data requirements, perhaps by leveraging unsupervised or self-supervised techniques. Investigating the transferability of the learned representations to even more diverse domains is another promising direction.
