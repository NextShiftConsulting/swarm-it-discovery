# Can AI Match Human Experts? Evaluating LLM-Generated Feedback on Resident Scholarly Projects

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.550 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.37/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** ai-safety

**Core Contribution**

This paper addresses the challenge of delivering timely and high-quality feedback on resident scholarly projects, particularly in large training programs. The key innovation is the development of an AI-assisted evaluation system, powered by the open-weight LLaMA 3.1 large language model (LLM), which can generate formative feedback on Family Medicine residents' scholarly projects. The authors compare the performance of this AI-generated feedback to that of expert human evaluators, aiming to assess whether AI can match the quality of human feedback.

The significance of this work lies in the potential to scale the delivery of feedback on resident projects, a labor-intensive task, by leveraging AI capabilities. Providing timely and high-quality feedback is crucial for the professional development of resident scholars, and automating this process could have a substantial impact on training programs.

**Technical Approach**

The authors developed an AI-assisted evaluation system that ingests heterogeneous resident submissions (PDFs, scans, photographs) via optical character recognition (OCR) and generates section-by-section feedback aligned with program rubrics. They conducted a three-phase study to evaluate the quality of the AI-generated feedback compared to expert human feedback.

In each phase, the researchers evaluated 80 feedback reports, with 40 generated by the AI system and 40 produced by research experts. The reports spanned four project types: Quality Improvement, Survey-Based, Research, and Literature Review. Blinded raters completed a 25-item survey across five constructs: understanding and reasoning, trust and confidence, quality of information, expression style and persona, and safety and harm.

The authors employed the open-weight LLaMA 3.1 LLM as the foundation for their AI-assisted evaluation system. They leveraged curated prompts to guide the model's generation of feedback aligned with the program's rubrics. The experimental setup allowed for a comprehensive comparison of the AI-generated and human-generated feedback across different project types and feedback formats (short, question and timeline, and final reports).

**Key Results**

The survey results revealed that human feedback generally outscored the AI-generated feedback, although the differences were smaller in some cases. For short reports, humans scored higher on quality and trust constructs. In final reports, the differences were smaller for quality and persona, while the AI was rated higher for the safety construct.

Interestingly, the performance of the AI system varied depending on the project type. In survey-based final reports, the AI scored higher on quality and safety compared to human evaluators. However, in quality improvement short reports, humans were markedly superior in the reasoning construct.

Overall, the results suggest that the open-weight LLM with curated prompts can generate rubric-aligned feedback at scale that approaches the quality of expert human feedback, particularly in selected contexts.

**Significance & Limitations**

This study demonstrates the potential of AI-assisted evaluation systems to augment and, in some cases, outperform human experts in providing feedback on resident scholarly projects. As newer and more capable open-weight language models are released, the performance of such systems is expected to improve further.

One limitation of the study is the reliance on a single LLM, the LLaMA 3.1, which may not be the most advanced or specialized model for this task. Exploring the use of other LLMs, potentially fine-tuned or adapted for the specific domain, could yield further insights.

Additionally, the study focused on a limited set of project types and feedback formats. Expanding the evaluation to a broader range of project types and feedback modalities, such as audio or video, would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the AI system's capabilities.

**RSCT Relevance**

This research is relevant to the RSCT (Representation, Safety, Composability, and Transparency) framework, particularly in the context of AI safety. The study explores the use of a large language model, LLaMA 3.1, to generate feedback on resident scholarly projects, which can be considered a proxy for human-generated content.

The authors' assessment of the safety and harm constructs in their evaluation provides insights into the potential risks and limitations of AI-generated feedback. The finding that the AI system was rated higher on the safety construct in certain contexts suggests that it may be able to provide more consistent and less harmful feedback compared to human evaluators.

This work also highlights the importance of transparency and interpretability in AI systems, as the authors have made their code and system prompts openly available. Transparency is a crucial aspect of AI safety, as it allows for better understanding, auditing, and monitoring of the system's behavior and outputs.

Overall, this research contributes to the ongoing efforts in the field of AI safety, particularly in the context of large language models and their applications in generating content that can impact human decision-making and learning processes.
