# An interpretable prototype parts-based neural network for medical tabular data

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.495 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.37/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** representation-learning

Title: Interpretable Prototype Parts-based Neural Network for Medical Tabular Data

1. **Core Contribution**

This paper proposes a novel neural network architecture for medical tabular data that aims to bridge the gap between predictive performance and interpretability. Specifically, the authors introduce an interpretable prototype parts-based model that learns meaningful prototypical parts from structured patient data. Unlike previous prototype-based models in computer vision, this approach is tailored to tabular medical records and employs trainable patching over patient features to learn human-readable prototypes.

The key innovation is the ability of this model to provide **interpretable concept-based predictions** by comparing a patient's features to the learned prototypes in the latent space of the network. This is crucial in healthcare domains, where not only accuracy but also the transparency and interpretability of model decisions are essential for building trust and enabling case-based reasoning by clinicians.

2. **Technical Approach**

The proposed model is inspired by the prototype parts-based deep neural networks developed for computer vision tasks. However, the authors adapt this approach to work with tabular medical data, which lacks the inherent spatial structure of images.

The core of the model is the **prototype parts module**, which learns a set of meaningful prototypical parts from the input features. These parts are represented as binary or discretized feature subsets, allowing the model to express prototypes in human-readable terms. The authors employ trainable **patching** over the input features to identify these prototypical parts, unlike the fixed spatial patches used in vision models.

To handle the structured nature of medical data, the authors also introduce a **discretization step** to transform continuous diagnostic result features into a set of discrete bins or categories. This discretization step is integrated into the model's architecture and is jointly trained with the rest of the network.

The authors evaluate their model on several medical benchmark datasets, including MIMIC-III and Diabetes 130-US hospitals. They compare the performance of their prototype parts-based model to widely used baseline models, such as logistic regression, random forests, and fully connected neural networks.

3. **Key Results**

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed prototype parts-based model achieves **classification performance competitive with the baseline models**, while also providing the desired interpretability. The authors report that their model can achieve **classification accuracy on par with the best-performing baselines** on the tested datasets.

More importantly, the authors show that the learned prototypical parts are **meaningful and aligned with clinical language**. By visualizing and analyzing the prototypes, they demonstrate that the model is able to capture clinically relevant patterns in the data, such as specific combinations of diagnostic features that are indicative of certain medical conditions.

4. **Significance & Limitations**

The significance of this work lies in its potential to **bridge the gap between predictive performance and interpretability** in clinical decision support systems. By providing interpretable concept-based predictions, the proposed model can help clinicians better understand and trust the model's decisions, ultimately improving their ability to make informed clinical judgments.

However, the authors acknowledge several limitations of their approach. First, the discretization of continuous features may lead to some information loss, and the impact of this step on model performance is not fully explored. Additionally, the interpretability of the model may be limited to the specific prototypes learned during training, and it may not be able to capture more complex or nuanced relationships in the data.

5. **RSCT Relevance**

This work is highly relevant to the goals of representation learning, as it focuses on learning interpretable and human-understandable representations from structured medical data. By capturing prototypical parts that are aligned with clinical language, the proposed model advances the field of **interpretable machine learning**, which is crucial for the safe and trustworthy deployment of AI systems in high-stakes domains like healthcare.

Furthermore, the prototype parts-based architecture and the joint learning of discretization and prototypes demonstrate the authors' attention to **model transparency and interpretability**, key considerations in AI safety and alignment research. The ability to explain model decisions in terms of clinically meaningful prototypes can help address the "black box" problem that often hinders the adoption of AI in healthcare.

Overall, this paper presents an important step towards developing **more interpretable and trustworthy AI systems** for medical applications, with potential implications for representation learning, AI safety, and the broader goal of aligning AI systems with human values and priorities.
