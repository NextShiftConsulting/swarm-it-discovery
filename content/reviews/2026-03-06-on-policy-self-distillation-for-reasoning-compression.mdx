# On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Compression

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.549 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.37/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** llm-agents

On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Compression

1. **Core Contribution**:
This paper tackles the problem of reducing the verbosity of large language models (LLMs) without compromising their reasoning capabilities. The key innovation is a novel method called On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Compression (OPSDC), which teaches models to reason more concisely by distilling their own concise behavior back into themselves. This is an important problem because LLMs often produce redundant and even harmful outputs, which can lead to compounding errors and inefficient use of computational resources.

The central insight of OPSDC is that much of what reasoning models produce is not just redundant, but actively detrimental to their performance. By conditioning the same model on a "be concise" instruction and minimizing the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the teacher's and student's logits, the authors are able to achieve substantial token reductions without sacrificing accuracy.

2. **Technical Approach**:
The OPSDC method is remarkably simple yet effective. It consists of two key steps:

1. **Teacher Logit Generation**: The authors condition the same LLM on a "be concise" instruction to obtain teacher logits, which represent the model's own concise behavior.
2. **Student Training**: The student model is trained to minimize the reverse KL divergence between its own logits and the teacher logits, effectively distilling the concise reasoning back into itself.

This self-distillation approach does not require any ground-truth answers, token budgets, or difficulty estimators - it relies solely on the model's own internal representations to learn to reason more concisely. The authors evaluated OPSDC on two large-scale language models, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B, using the MATH-500 and AIME 2024 datasets.

For the MATH-500 benchmark, the authors found that OPSDC achieved 57-59% token reduction while improving accuracy by 9-16 percentage points absolute. On the AIME 2024 dataset, the 14B model gained 10 percentage points in accuracy with 41% compression. Importantly, the method was able to automatically adjust the level of compression based on the difficulty of the problem, aggressively compressing easy problems while preserving the necessary deliberation for hard ones.

3. **Key Results**:
The key findings of this paper are:
* OPSDC achieves substantial token reductions of 57-59% on MATH-500 and 41% on AIME 2024 while improving accuracy.
* The method is able to automatically balance the level of compression based on problem difficulty, preserving deliberation for hard problems while aggressively compressing easy ones.
* OPSDC is a simple yet surprisingly sophisticated approach that does not require any additional supervision, token budgets, or difficulty estimators.

4. **Significance & Limitations**:
The significance of this work lies in its potential to improve the efficiency and performance of large language models. By reducing the verbosity of model outputs without compromising their reasoning capabilities, OPSDC could lead to more compact and interpretable models, as well as more efficient use of computational resources.

One potential limitation of the approach is that it may not generalize as well to more open-ended or creative tasks, where the "be concise" instruction may not capture the desired output characteristics. Additionally, the method's ability to automatically adjust the level of compression based on problem difficulty may not extend to all types of tasks or datasets.

Future work could explore ways to make OPSDC more flexible and applicable to a wider range of problems, perhaps by incorporating additional guidance or constraints beyond the "be concise" instruction. Investigating the generalizability of the method to different types of tasks and datasets would also be a valuable direction for further research.
