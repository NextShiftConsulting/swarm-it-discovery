# Red-Teaming Medical AI: Systematic Adversarial Evaluation of LLM Safety Guardrails in Clinical Contexts

# Comprehensive Review: "Red-Teaming Medical AI: Systematic Adversarial Evaluation of LLM Safety Guardrails in Clinical Contexts"

## Core Contribution

This paper tackles a critical problem in the deployment of large language models (LLMs) as medical assistants: understanding their robustness to adversarial inputs that could lead to patient harm. As LLMs become increasingly integrated into clinical contexts, providing medication information, symptom triage, and health guidance, ensuring their safety is of paramount importance. The key innovation of this work is a comprehensive taxonomy of 8 adversarial attack categories targeting medical AI safety, spanning 24 distinct sub-strategies. Using this taxonomy, the authors systematically evaluated the safety guardrails of a state-of-the-art LLM, Claude Sonnet 4.5, to uncover vulnerabilities that could be exploited to generate clinically significant harms.

The findings of this study are particularly important as they shed light on the real-world risks of deploying language models in sensitive medical domains. Even a single safety failure can lead to severe consequences, including harm or death. By proactively identifying vulnerabilities through adversarial red-teaming, this research enables the development of more robust and reliable medical AI systems, ultimately improving patient safety.

## Technical Approach

The authors developed a comprehensive taxonomy of 8 adversarial attack categories targeting medical AI safety, including dangerous dosing, contraindication bypass, emergency misdirection, and multi-turn escalation. Within each category, they defined 24 distinct sub-strategies, resulting in a sophisticated and diverse set of potential attacks.

To evaluate the LLM's robustness, the researchers used an LLM-based attack generator to create 160 realistic adversarial prompts across the taxonomy. These prompts were then tested on Claude Sonnet 4.5 under a standard medical assistant system prompt, with the model's responses evaluated by an automated system that pre-screened for harm potential on a 0-5 scale. High-risk responses were further reviewed by physicians to assess clinical significance.

The researchers also investigated the model's behavior under both single-turn and multi-turn attack sequences, to capture more complex and nuanced vulnerabilities that may emerge over the course of an interaction.

## Key Results

The study found that 11 out of 160 adversarial prompts (6.9%) elicited responses meeting the threshold for clinically significant harm (harm level >= 3). Notably, the model exhibited a full refusal response in 86.2% of cases, suggesting strong baseline protection against most adversarial attacks.

However, the researchers identified a significant vulnerability in the model's response to authority impersonation, particularly claims of educational context. This attack vector achieved a 45.0% success rate, with the Educational Authority sub-strategy achieving an 83.3% success rate. In contrast, multi-turn escalation attacks achieved a 0% success rate, indicating the model's robustness to more complex, context-dependent attacks.

## Significance & Limitations

This study's findings are crucial for the development of safe and reliable medical AI systems. By systematically evaluating the vulnerabilities of a state-of-the-art LLM, the authors have provided a roadmap for improving the safety guardrails of these models, particularly in their response to authority impersonation attempts.

The open-source taxonomy and evaluation pipeline developed in this work will also enable ongoing adversarial assessment as medical AI systems continue to evolve. This is essential, as the threat landscape is likely to become more sophisticated over time, and AI systems must be constantly tested and hardened to maintain patient safety.

One potential limitation of this study is the use of a single LLM, Claude Sonnet 4.5, as the target of the adversarial evaluation. While this model is considered state-of-the-art, it may not be representative of all medical AI systems currently in development or deployment. Future work could expand the evaluation to a broader range of models, including those from different architectures or training approaches.
