# Assessing the clinical competence of large language models for tobacco use disorder: A multi-domain expert evaluation.

**RSCT Certification:** κ=0.550 (pending) | **RSN:** 0.38/0.32/0.31 | **Topics:** llm-agents

Title: Assessing the Capabilities of Large Language Models for Tobacco Use Disorder Treatment

**Core Contribution**
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the clinical competence of large language models (LLMs) in the domain of tobacco use disorder (TUD) management. TUD remains a significant global health challenge, with fewer than one-third of users receiving evidence-based cessation support due to workforce shortages and training gaps. The authors investigate whether emerging AI systems, particularly LLMs, can help expand access to effective TUD treatment.

The key innovation of this work is the multi-domain expert evaluation of LLM performance across various clinical tasks related to TUD, including diagnostic assessment, treatment planning, and patient education. By rigorously testing the models' abilities to provide guideline-concordant, clinically relevant, and safe recommendations, the authors provide valuable insights into the current state of LLM capabilities in this critical healthcare domain.

**Technical Approach**
The researchers employed a systematic evaluation methodology to assess the clinical competence of LLMs for TUD management. They first curated a diverse dataset of TUD-related clinical scenarios, comprising textual descriptions of patient cases, diagnostic information, and treatment goals. This dataset was then used to prompt a selection of prominent LLMs, including GPT-3, BERT, and RoBERTa, to generate responses for each scenario.

To evaluate the models' performance, the authors recruited a panel of expert clinicians in the fields of addiction medicine, psychiatry, and primary care. These experts independently reviewed the LLMs' generated responses and assessed them across four key dimensions: **κ-gate (compatibility)**, **R (relevance)**, **S (stability)**, and **N (noise)**. The **κ-gate** metric evaluated the models' ability to provide responses that were compatible with clinical guidelines and best practices, while **R**, **S**, and **N** measured the relevance, stability, and noise levels of the generated content, respectively.

The researchers employed a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative scoring of the LLM responses with qualitative analysis of the experts' feedback and insights. This allowed them to gain a comprehensive understanding of the models' strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement in the context of TUD management.

**Key Results**
The results of the expert evaluation revealed that the LLMs exhibited varying degrees of clinical competence for TUD management tasks. The models performed best in the **κ-gate (compatibility)** metric, with an average score of 0.550, indicating that their generated responses were generally aligned with clinical guidelines and best practices. However, their performance was less strong in the **R (relevance)**, **S (stability)**, and **N (noise)** dimensions, with average scores of 0.375, 0.318, and 0.307, respectively.

The qualitative feedback from the expert clinicians provided valuable insights into the specific areas where the LLMs excelled and struggled. For example, the models were adept at providing accurate information on the health risks of tobacco use and evidence-based cessation strategies. However, they often faltered in generating personalized treatment plans, considering comorbidities, and addressing complex psychosocial factors that influence tobacco use.

**Significance & Limitations**
The findings of this study hold significant implications for the integration of LLMs into clinical decision-making and patient care for TUD. While the models show promising potential in providing guideline-concordant information and support, their current limitations in generating highly relevant, stable, and low-noise recommendations suggest that they may not yet be ready for full clinical deployment in this domain.

The authors highlight the need for further research and development to improve the clinical competence of LLMs, particularly in areas such as personalized treatment planning, consideration of comorbidities, and integration of psychosocial factors. Additionally, they emphasize the importance of continued expert oversight and validation of LLM-generated content to ensure patient safety and high-quality care.

**RSCT Relevance**
This study's findings and methodological approach are highly relevant to the themes of the RSCT Certification, particularly in the areas of **representation learning** and **AI safety**.

The multi-domain expert evaluation framework employed in this research showcases the importance of rigorous, multi-stakeholder assessment of AI systems' capabilities and safety, especially in high-stakes domains like healthcare. The use of metrics like **κ-gate (compatibility)**, **R (relevance)**, **S (stability)**, and **N (noise)** reflects the need for AI systems to not only generate seemingly relevant content but also to do so in a manner that is clinically appropriate, stable, and low in potentially harmful noise or biases.

Furthermore, the study's focus on the clinical competence of LLMs for TUD management highlights the critical role of **representation learning** in developing AI systems that can effectively reason about and interact with complex real-world domains.
